About Daily Bread

Become a disciplined student of the whole Bible.

One of a kind

Daily Bread is a Messianic one-year Bible reading plan, the only one of its kind. It gives you a daily, balanced diet from the Word of God and takes you through the entire Bible in a year. As the Master said, “It is written: ‘Man shall not live on bread alone, but on every word that proceeds out of the mouth of God’” (Matthew 4:4; Deuteronomy 8:3).

The Bible has come to us from the Jewish people, and Daily Bread reads it the way Israel does:

  • It follows the same Jewish division of the Scriptures that the Messiah and the Apostles knew: the Torah, the Prophets and the Writings (Luke 24:44), together with the Apostles.
  • It takes you through a portion from each of these sections every day.
  • It follows the same Torah portions that all of Israel studies each week, and completes them with the rest of the biblical text.
  • It walks through the year by the Hebrew calendar, with its Sabbaths, Feasts and new moons, and what has happened in the history of Israel on different days.

How the readings work

Every day except Sabbaths and holidays has four readings: one from the Torah (the Five Books of Moses), one from the Prophets, one from the Writings and one from the Apostles.

The weekly Torah portion, the Parashah, is read in every synagogue around the world each Sabbath, and the Apostles encouraged disciples who were not Jewish to follow the synagogue’s Scripture readings (Acts 15:21). Each portion is divided into seven shorter readings, the aliyot, which make up the daily Torah reading. On the Sabbath the seventh aliyah is read together with the accompanying portion from the Prophets, the Haftarah (Luke 4:16), and Daily Bread adds a Messianic reading from the Apostles on Sabbaths and holidays.

We recommend reading all the day’s portions early in the morning. If time does not allow, read the rest in the evening, or read two portions in the morning and two in the evening. If you fall behind, you can catch up on the Sabbath, but a disciplined daily reading of the Word is best.

The reading plan was prepared by Lars Enarson and is published by Ariel Media, the publishing ministry of The Watchman International. The present year, 5787, runs from 4 October 2026 to 24 October 2027.

Reading here

Daily Bread Online is free. Each day’s readings are given in the Berean Standard Bible, with the Hebrew and Greek text, word study for every Hebrew and Greek word, maps for key passages, the Jewish calendar and Shabbat times.

At the end of each day’s reading you can mark it as read. The calendar then shows the days you have read, and if you miss a few days the site offers to help you catch up, or simply to pick up again from today. Your progress is kept in this browser on this device only. Nothing is sent to us, and clearing your browser data erases it.

Paper and pen

A screen is a good place to look things up. For reading to understand, paper still does better: studies comparing the two have found that people understand longer texts better when they read them in print than on a screen. Writing helps too. Putting what you read into your own words, by hand, engages the mind more deeply than typing and helps you remember it.

That is what the printed Daily Bread journal is for. It follows the same plan, with a page for each day: the Gregorian and Hebrew dates, the day’s readings, notes on the Hebrew calendar and on what happened in the Bible or in history on that day, and empty lines to record what speaks to you from the Scriptures, prayer subjects and answers to prayer. It is a hardcover book, in Classic and Women’s editions, with Daily Bread for Kids for younger readers.
